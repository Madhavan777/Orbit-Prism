01 · The bottleneck in numbers

Each pass carries 19.2 MB, so the satellite must choose.

A 256 kbit/s link open for ten minutes moves 19.2 MB, and one gigabyte would need about 52 passes. Meanwhile, conventional Earth observation satellites waste link bandwidth transmitting cloud-obscured or non-critical frames while urgent disaster perimeters wait in flash memory. We read this as a choice problem, not only a compression problem: decide what goes first, and state honestly how many critical tiles might be missed.

19.2 MB

Pass transmission budget

Total data a LEO satellite can downlink across a 10-minute ground station window at 256 kbit/s.

52 Passes

Per gigabyte needed

Without on-device triage, transmitting an uncompressed sensor swath takes days across multiple orbital passes.

4.2 MB

Onboard SRAM ceiling

Strict physical memory limit. Entire chips (6.8 MB) cannot fit; inference must run tile-by-tile in INT8.

4.8×

Faster critical alert

Priority L0/L2 scheduling transmits validated flood perimeters to emergency operators in under 90 seconds.

    06 · Defense & Architecture

    Questions judges ask.

    Direct, unvarnished engineering answers to common evaluation questions.

    Is this just cloud detection?
    No. Cloud detection is simply an initial gating sensor input. The core contribution is the combination of calibrated miss-rate control, automated radar fallback when optical bands are obscured, and a byte-budget pass scheduler measured together as a complete edge system.
    Why tiles, not pixels?
    A full satellite image chip (6.8 MB uncompressed) exceeds the physical 4.2 MB SRAM limit of candidate spaceborne microcontrollers. Tiling into 128×128 sub-blocks allows tile-by-tile streaming inference within memory bounds, which is sufficient to determine transmission priority.
    Why a small CNN over a water index?
    A colour-index baseline (such as NDWI / MNDWI) is one of our primary benchmark comparisons. If the small CNN does not demonstrate a measurable recall advantage over the simple index on held-out test data, we state that explicitly in the results table.
    How does it fit in 4.2 MB?
    Tiles are processed sequentially one at a time. Model weights are quantized to INT8, and peak activation memory is derived directly from the neural network execution graph (see the onboard memory table).
    What if radar is wrong?
    Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) can generate false alarms over smooth terrain, flat runways, or specular reflectors. Uncertain tiles are scheduled as lower-priority preview packets rather than discarded, and false alarm rates are tracked and reported in the metrics.
    What if the cloud check is wrong?
    If thin cirrus clouds are misclassified as clear sky, optical false dismissals could occur; conversely, overestimating clouds incurs the cost of activating the radar pipeline. We measure and report cloud triage error rates separately from segmentation error.
    Does the promise hold under data shift?
    The calibrated miss rate holds when test data resembles calibration data. When encountering novel terrain, severe atmospheric disturbance, or unseen geographies, performance can degrade. We report the empirical miss rate observed on completely held-out flood events.
    Do optical and radar come from one satellite?
    No. In this benchmark, optical imagery comes from Sentinel-2 and radar imagery comes from Sentinel-1. The co-registered paired payload is simulated to evaluate future dual-payload or collaborative orbital constellations.
    Is this real hardware?
    No. This is an engineering simulation using measured model footprints, verified INT8 quantization parameters, and estimated peak SRAM activations benchmarked against target space-qualified processor specs.
    Why only floods?
    Floods represent our completed worked example with verified ground truth from the Sen1Floods11 dataset. Wildfire and burn-scar pipelines are architecturally planned but have not yet been ingested into the evaluation pipeline.
    07 · Technical Definitions & Limits

    Glossary

    Formal definitions of mission and algorithmic terminology.

    Tile

    A 128×128 sub-region of a satellite image chip processed as an individual unit through the onboard inference pipeline.

    Chip

    A 512×512 pixel satellite scene covering approximately 26 square kilometers on the ground, composed of a 4×4 grid of 16 tiles.

    Critical Tile

    A tile containing validated flood inundation across at least 30% of its surface area according to hand-labelled ground truth.

    Miss-Rate Dial

    A configurable parameter (1%, 5%, 10%) determining the operational decision threshold calculated via conformal risk calibration.

    Scaled Pass

    A 3.03 MB transmission budget representing 20% of the event data, used for benchmarking pass scheduling under link constraints.

    L0 / L1 / L2

    L2 represents immediate 32-byte alert packets; L1 represents compressed preview thumbnails; L0 represents full uncompressed raw pixel data.

    Priority Score

    A composite metric combining flood severity probability, critical flag, and sensor confidence used to sequence downlink queueing.

    Held-Out Event

    A completely unseen geographic flood disaster (e.g. Bolivia) withheld during calibration to evaluate performance under real-world domain shifts.

    Simulated Cloud

    Synthetic cloud masks blended over optical imagery to systematically evaluate the automated routing switch to radar.

    SRAM

    Static Random-Access Memory on the satellite microcontroller, constrained to 4.2 MB physical capacity.

    INT8

    8-bit integer quantization used to compress deep neural network weights and activations for edge execution.

    Honest Limits & Operational Scope

    • Calibrated miss rates hold when test data resembles calibration data; unseen geographic biomes may experience distribution shift.
    • Paired optical (Sentinel-2) and radar (Sentinel-1) swaths are simulated from co-registered public datasets, not an integrated single-satellite sensor.
    • Cloud detection errors directly influence sensor routing: undetected clouds degrade optical inference, while overdetected clouds trigger unnecessary radar processing.
    • Memory footprints and execution timings are based on INT8 graph analysis and simulation on representative hardware, not on-orbit flight qualification.
    • Currently validated solely on riverine flood disaster scenarios; forest fire and burn-scar datasets are not yet loaded.
